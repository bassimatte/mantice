Build a drone that moves without hurrying.

A convincing ambient drone is less about adding more oscillators and more about giving a few layers different jobs, different timescales, and enough headroom to breathe.

Open Simple Drone Open the instrument

A reliable four-part method

  1. Begin with one low anchor. Use FM or subtractive synthesis around 45–110 Hz. Keep its modulation gentle. A stable foundation makes later movement feel intentional.
  2. Add contrast, not duplication. Place a second layer one or two octaves higher, with a different synthesis engine or harmonic ratio. Reduce its level before adding more voices.
  3. Use drone-speed motion. Filter LFO and spatial movement should unfold over seconds or minutes. Start below 0.1 Hz for filter motion and below 0.005 for Mantice spatial speed.
  4. Create depth in stages. First set layer width and position, then reverb, then shimmer or modulation. Listen in mono before increasing width.

What to listen for

Weight

The low layer should be audible without using all the headroom. Infrasonic energy can feel quiet while limiting the whole mix.

Evolution

Movement should reveal itself gradually. If the modulation is obvious immediately, halve its rate before reducing its depth.

Separation

Give layers distinct octaves, spectra, or positions. More voices do not automatically create a larger drone.

Headroom

Judge loudness after the texture is balanced. Mantice protects peaks; it does not use a blanket gain increase to disguise spectral problems.

Good starting presets

Deep Hum demonstrates weight; Cosmic Expanse demonstrates density; and Cathedral Ascension demonstrates shimmer and height.
